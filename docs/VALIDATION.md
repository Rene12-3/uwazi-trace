# Validation

## The honest problem
There is no ground truth for "this award was corrupt". Prosecutions, audit queries and tribunal outcomes are selective, delayed and cover a tiny fraction of events.
"Accuracy" is therefore the wrong metric. The goal is **useful triage** and **discovery of data errors**, measured by expert judgement.

## Base rates matter
Suppose 100,000 awards, 3% with a real issue, a detector with 60% sensitivity and 95% specificity.
It flags about 1,800 true and 4,850 false awards, so **precision (PPV) is about 27%**. That is acceptable for deciding where to look and unacceptable for accusing anyone.
This is why the product is a triage tool, and why every alert carries innocent explanations.

## Validation plan
1. **Synthetic injection:** plant known patterns in a copy of the data and measure recall and false positives per detector. *Implemented.*
2. **Expert blind review:** two or three auditors or procurement practitioners rate a sample of alerts as "worth follow-up", "explained" or "data error". Report precision@k, usefulness rate and Cohen's kappa. *Planned.*
3. **Weak-label backtests:** compare indicators with audit findings and procurement review board outcomes for the same entity-year, interpreting lift, not accuracy (labels are biased).
4. **Calibration:** do nominal price intervals contain the expected share of held-out prices? *Implemented on clean synthetic data.*
5. **Temporal backtest:** compute indicators using only data available at time t.
6. **Component metrics:** entity-resolution pairwise precision/recall on a double-labelled gold sample; OCR exact-match on amounts.
7. **Stability:** alert churn week on week and rank stability under weight perturbation.
8. **Fairness audit (quarterly, published):** flag rates by county size, region and category, adjusted for coverage and size.

## Current results (synthetic benchmark, seed 7, 3,049 awards)

| Planted pattern | Planted | Found | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier concentration | 25 | 25 | 100% |
| Splitting | 24 | 24 | 100% |
| Bunching below threshold | 25 | 25 | 100% |
| Very short bid windows | 20 | 20 | 100% |
| Inflated unit prices | 15 | 7 | 47% |

Output: 121 alerts from 3,049 awards (6 tier 1, 26 tier 2, 89 tier 3). Supplier name resolution reduces 362 messy names to the true 120.

**Read this carefully.** The data is clean, random and generated by the same assumptions the detectors use, so these numbers are an upper bound, not a forecast.
The price detector is deliberately conservative. Real-world performance is unknown until step 2 above is done on real data.
These checks run on every commit in `tests/`.
